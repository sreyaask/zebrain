0.3Executive summary

Zebrain lets a person describe a job in plain words and get back a small language model of their own: trained on their own free Kaggle GPU, tested automatically, published to their own Hugging Face account, chat-ready with an API, and fixable when it answers wrong, without writing code. Verified

Where the company stands

  • Product: a working end-to-end pipeline (plan → data checks → baseline test → QLoRA training → retest → publish → chat/API → fix → Gemini-led "Testify" improvement) built with Next.js, Supabase, the Kaggle API, Hugging Face and Gemini [1]. Verified
  • Evidence it works: internal test models (an SQL assistant and a coding assistant, both 1.5B) were trained, published, served and called over the API. The coding model's exam score moved 73% → 78% across rounds while its held-out test score moved 38% → 42%: real but modest gains that show small models need better data, not just more rounds. Verified
  • Not built yet: payments, paid GPU training, always-on hosting, the Business service workflow, production deployment, a booking page (the link is a placeholder). Verified

The bet

Fine-tuning is now cheap, but it is still a developer's activity: datasets, chat templates, LoRA settings, evaluation and hosting each take expertise. Zebrain's wager is that owning a small, specialised model (named, on-brand, on-topic, portable) is valuable to small businesses and makers who will never touch a notebook, and that a free first model on the user's own GPU is the cheapest possible way to reach them.

Illustrative

In the base-case model in Chapter 3, Zebrain reaches about ₹1.9 lakh monthly revenue by month 12 at a 65% gross margin, while still burning about ₹1.6 lakh per month. Break-even needs about ₹4.4 lakh monthly revenue at that cost base, so the plan needs about ₹25 lakh of outside funding or faster revenue by month 6. These are hypothetical figures that show the method. They are not forecasts of actual results.
